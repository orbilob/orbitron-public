# 🛰️ The chain of three

Three parts, in one direction, with nothing flowing back.

> **The sonde brings the material. The assay says whether there is anything in
> it. Mission control gives the finding an identity and puts it to work.**

Each part has a name, a job, and — the point of naming them at all — **something
it is forbidden to do**. A part that both collects and judges its own collection
has no way to be wrong out loud.

---

## 📡 ORPROBE — *the sonde*

Goes out, brings samples back, and **computes nothing**. That last clause is the
whole design: a collector that also interprets is a collector whose mistakes are
invisible, because the interpretation hides them.

| Process | Designation | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **The Sampler** | `Proxima Centauri b` | takes a sample of the market every minute — fetch, validate, store |
| **The Sweep** | `TOI-700 d` | sweeps the world: feeds, macro releases, on-chain values |
| **The Store** | `Ross 128 b` | keeps it raw, exactly as it arrived |

The names come from real, catalogued exoplanets. `Proxima Centauri b` is the
nearest one known — the closest thing we reach. `TOI-700 d` was found by a
survey mission that sweeps the whole sky. `Ross 128 b` orbits an unusually quiet
star: in the store, nothing changes.

---

## 🧪 ORLAB — *the assay*

An assay is the test that determines what is **actually** in the ore. Most
assays come back saying *nothing valuable* — and that is not a failure of the
assay. It is the assay working.

| Process | Designation | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **The Inspection** | `Teegarden's Star b` | checks the data **before** anyone analyses it |
| **The Cut** | `TRAPPIST-1e` | cuts the qualifying events out of the stream |
| ⭐ **The Verdict** | `K2-18b` | decides whether there is an edge at all — and usually decides there is none |

**The Verdict** is the part worth reading twice. It declusters overlapping
events, builds a baseline that does not overlap itself, charges the cost of
acting, and corrects for having asked the same question several ways
(Benjamini–Hochberg). Every one of those exists to make a positive result
*harder* to obtain.

`K2-18b` is the planet where water vapour was detected — a finding that then had
to survive re-examination. That is the job.

## ⚙️ ORCORE — *mission control*

Where a proven method is given an identity and put to work.

| Process | Designation | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **The Passport Desk** | `Kepler-22b` | a pattern with no identity is not admitted |
| ⭐ **The Press** | `LHS 1140 b` | one machine, never modified for a new pattern |
| **The Harvest** | `Kepler-452b` | comes back later and records what actually happened |

**The Press** is the idea the whole repository is built around: there is exactly
one machine every pattern passes through, and **it is not touched when a new
pattern arrives**. A press that gets adjusted per pattern is not a press — it is
another analysis, and nothing measured through it can be compared with anything
else.

`LHS 1140 b` is a massive rocky super-Earth: dense, heavy, immovable.

**The Harvest** exists because a pattern speaks at 16:43 and is proved right or
wrong at 17:43 — and it cannot sit and wait, it has to watch the next minute. So
a separate process returns when the answer has ripened and writes it down.

## 🎓 And then: four stages

What leaves the press is a bare signal. Four named stages stand between it and
anything worth acting on:

| # | Stage | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **lost in space** | the signal exists, and it is naked — no context, no weight |
| 2 | **found home** | it is dressed in the context of the day it was born in |
| 3 | **back to school** | it is weighed, judged, and packaged into a fixed format |
| 4 | **the proving ground** | the finished, packaged signal is put to the test |

Stage 3 ends in a **document**, not a function call — a small fixed record with a
version, which anything downstream reads. That boundary is deliberate: a format
can be written down, read back six months later and compared. A function call
can only be run.

## 🔢 Nine processes, nine planets

The reserve of catalogued scientific exoplanets held exactly nine usable names.
Three parts wanted three each. The list is now spent, and that is recorded
rather than quietly topped up: **a tenth process will need a decision, not the
next name off a list.**

There is a second reserve, of planets carrying mythological and cultural names,
and it is kept for something else entirely — the
[pattern classes](../02%20%E2%80%94%20THE%20RESEARCH/Market%20regimes.md). The
split is not decoration:

> A process is an instrument, and gets a catalogue name.
> A class is a hypothesis, and gets a name with a story.

---

## ⚠️ What is not being claimed

- **These nine names are new.** They exist in this vault and in the project's
  naming record — **not in the code**. Not a single module has been renamed.
- **More than thirty other processes run in these three parts** and have no
  public name at all. They are not listed here, and inventing names for them
  would be worse than leaving them unnamed.
- **The four stages are designed, not built.** Stage 1 is partial and stage 4 has
  never run on anything. No signal has ever been put to the test.
- **Nothing here has been proven profitable**, because nothing here has been
  proven at all. The measure at this stage is honesty.

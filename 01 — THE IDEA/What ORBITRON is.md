# 🛰️ What ORBITRON is

A personal research project about **market mechanics** — what moves prices, and how
a trend is born, sustained and dies. On every scale: from twenty minutes to a
quarter. The first market studied is bitcoin.

---

## The sentence that explains the rest

> **Trading is the test, not the goal.**

A pattern that makes money is *evidence that some mechanism was understood
correctly*. That is why a live chain with real risk management is being built at
all. But the understanding stays the point: a system that wins without knowing why
is luck with good marketing.

This single sentence explains most of the decisions in this vault — why negative
results are recorded and kept, why a format is frozen before the logic that fills
it, why a backtest engine is built to be able to say *no*.

---

## What it actually does

```
watch the market  →  notice something  →  ask what kind of day it is
      →  decide: trade or not  →  hand it to a bot  →  write the day down forever
```

Six steps, each with a name and a boundary. [[The chain]] walks through them.

---

## Bitcoin is the first asset, not the only one

The architecture keeps the collector and the core as the stable base, while the
research side stays deliberately fluid. Over time it may look at different windows,
different methods and different assets. The system is designed to be **re-tuned**,
not to follow one fixed model forever.

---

## Who runs it

One person — **Joro the Best** — with an AI agent doing the reading, drafting and
implementation under written rules. The operator decides; the agent proposes and
executes after approval. That arrangement is documented in
[[Working with an AI agent]], because it is a real part of how this project is
built and hiding it would make the rest less honest.

---

## What makes it worth reading

Not the results — there are none to boast about yet, and that is stated plainly
everywhere in this vault.

What is worth reading is the **discipline**: decisions recorded with a date and a
reason, evidence tagged as verified / claimed / unknown, a rule that one statement
lives in exactly one place, and a script that refuses a commit when the vault
contradicts itself.

# ⚡ The SCALP philosophy

> [!success] The core idea of the whole project, in one paragraph
> We do not look for long-term positions. We listen, watch, analyse — and at the
> right moment we **ride the wave**: catch the volatility, determine direction, and
> scalp with leverage inside a short window (an hour or two), under strict risk
> management.

The trading side of ORBITRON has one name and one shape: **SCALP**.

---

## Three consequences that stopped being accidental

Once the philosophy was written down, three things across the project turned out to
be *implications* rather than choices:

| | What | Why it follows |
|---|---|---|
| ⏱️ | **The 60-minute window** | It is the hypothesis itself, not a convenient parameter. The claim being tested is that a repeatable fingerprint exists in the first minutes of a peak hourly move |
| 📈 | **Futures and leverage only** | A scalp on spot, unleveraged, is not the thing being tested |
| 🎯 | **Honesty as the success metric** | At this stage the question is "is the measurement true", not "how much did it make" |

Writing the philosophy down did not create these three. It **revealed** that they
were already load-bearing, and that arguing about them separately had been arguing
about the same thing three times.

---

## Why the shortest scale is where it starts

The project explicitly believes the same mechanism can appear at several scales.
If "seller exhaustion" is a real thing, it should leave a fingerprint in 60 minutes
*and* in 60 days — with different strength and different noise.

So why start at the smallest?

> **Feedback speed.** A hypothesis about 20 minutes can be checked against thousands
> of cases in minutes. A hypothesis about a quarterly trend has maybe thirty
> observations since 2019 — and thirty observations prove nothing.

That is the entire reason. Not that the short scale matters more.

---

## What "strict risk management" means here

Two things that are easy to say and easy to get backwards:

- **Leverage is a result, not a setting.** Position size is computed from *risk per
  trade* and *distance to the stop*. Leverage is what falls out of that arithmetic.
  A fixed leverage table would bypass exactly the calculation it exists to make.
- **Cost is not deducted from profit — it raises how often you must be right.** The
  fee floor is treated as a threshold on accuracy, not a subtraction at the end.

---

## The scales, and the question each one asks

| Scale | The question it asks |
|---|---|
| Minutes–hours | How a move forms from the inside |
| Days | What context was present when the move happened |
| Weeks–months | How a trend is born, sustained and dies |
| Quarters and up | Which regimes exist at all, and what switches them |

The system is scale-neutral by construction: the collector writes raw minutes, and
everything larger is aggregated from them. Nothing in the measuring machinery asks
whether a "bar" means a minute or a week.
